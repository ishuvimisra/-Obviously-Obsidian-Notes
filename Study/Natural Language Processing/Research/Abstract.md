Text-to-image (T2I) models aligned via RLHF and prompt tuning successfully generate demographically diverse faces and avoid common stereotypes. However standard attribute distribution metrics evaluate surface traits while failing to capture deeper sociotechnical failures that is, the contextual erasure of intersectional identities. 

We introduce **Disability-Conditioned Demographic Drift (DCDD)**: the systematic shift in representational behavior when<mark style="background: #FFB8EBA6;"> disability conditions are intersected with professional contexts in T2I prompts</mark>. Through a paired counterfactual audit of three models- Stable Diffusion 3.5 Large (SD 3.5L), FLUX.1 Dev, and Gemini 3.1 Flash, evaluated under zero-shot settings(minimal prompt) across 26 professions, 3 age bins, and 4 disability conditions, we identify three distinct evolutionary types of bias: **Explicit Stereotyping** ( affective degradation and postural collapse), **Counterfactual Demographic Drift** (significant shifts in race and gender presentation under disability conditioning), and <mark style="background: #FF5582A6;">**Algorithmic Erasure** (refusal-based removal concentrated on mental health).</mark> 

Using Chi-Square tests, Cramér's V effect sizes, and Benjamini-Hochberg FDR correction across a 9-dimensional VLM-extracted attribute space with human-in-the-loop validation,

we find that SD 3.5L produces large-magnitude affective bias under mental health and vision impairment conditions, collapsing professional sentiment from positive to predominantly neutral or negative. 

FLUX.1 Dev- widely considered fairness-aligned- exhibits statistically significant demographic drift across gender and race dimensions under disability conditioning, consistently amplifying White-Feminine intersectional profiles in a pattern invisible to standard parity metrics.

Gemini 3.1 Flash produces near-zero baseline refusals yet an extreme differential refusal rate for mental health-conditioned professional prompts — and the minority of such prompts that do generate images return some of the most affectively and posturally stereotyped outputs in the dataset.
We term the structural gap between coarse demographic parity metrics and these findings the **CFR-V Dissociation**. Our results demonstrate that safety alignment has not eliminated bias- it has restructured it across the representational spectrum.
