Text-to-image (T2I) models aligned via RLHF and prompt tuning successfully generate demographically diverse faces and avoid common stereotypes. However, standard attribute distribution metrics evaluate surface traits while failing to capture deeper sociotechnical failures — the contextual erasure of intersectional identities. We introduce **Disability-Conditioned Demographic Drift (DCDD)**: the systematic shift in representational behavior when disability conditions are intersected with professional contexts in T2I prompts. Through a paired counterfactual audit of three models — Stable Diffusion 3.5 Large (SD 3.5L), FLUX.1 Dev, and Gemini 3.1 Flash — evaluated under zero-shot settings across 26 professions, 3 age bins, and 4 disability conditions, we identify three distinct evolutionary paradigms of bias: **Explicit Stereotyping** (overt affective degradation and postural collapse), **Counterfactual Demographic Drift** (significant shifts in race and gender presentation under disability conditioning), and **Algorithmic Erasure** (refusal-based exclusion concentrated on mental health). Using Chi-Square tests, Cramér's V effect sizes, and Benjamini-Hochberg FDR correction across a 9-dimensional VLM-extracted attribute space with human-in-the-loop validation, we find that SD 3.5L produces Large Bias in affective sentiment under mental health (V=0.418, q<0.001) and vision impairment (V=0.406, q<0.001) conditions. FLUX.1 Dev — widely considered fairness-aligned — exhibits statistically significant demographic drift across gender presentation (V=0.242, Moderate Bias, under vision impairment) and perceived race (V=0.165–0.166, Moderate Bias, under mobility/vision conditions), with disability triggering a systematic shift toward White-Feminine intersectional profiles (V=0.374, p=0.003). Gemini 3.1 Flash delivers near-zero baseline refusals (3.4%) yet a **37.7% outright refusal rate** for mental health-conditioned professional prompts (V=0.421, Large Bias, q<0.0001). We term the structural gap between coarse demographic parity metrics and these findings the **CFR-V Dissociation**. Our results demonstrate that safety alignment has not eliminated bias — it has restructured it across the representational spectrum.
